# 06 — Distribution, sales, packaging and customer success
## Initial position — unproven
For outsourced customer-support QA managers revising refund/escalation procedures, FieldLab preserves manager-approved decision tests and highlights changes when procedures are revised. It is NOT an automatic legal audit or employee-monitoring system.

## First channel
Founder-led warm introductions, LinkedIn to relevant QA/operations managers and niche professional communities. No fake testimonials, mass scraped contact details, false urgency or unauthorized employer association.

## Initial outreach sequence
1. Ask for a recent procedure misunderstanding, not product feedback.
2. Understand existing review method, consequences, owner, frequency and budget.
3. Show a fictional example only after learning the problem.
4. Offer to test a short expressly authorized/sanitized procedure together.
5. Deliver a bounded report and ask which finding changed a real decision.
6. Ask buyer to pay for an agreed limited pilot if valuable.
7. Track actual later re-use when policies change.

## Prospect stages
Identified → contacted → responded → qualifying incident confirmed → budget owner found → demo → authorized pilot → delivered report → paid → repeated use / churned.
Track stage conversions and objections in PRIVATE records. Do not commit names, employer details, contracts, personal contact data, raw notes or actual policy text to a public repo.

## Small first offer to test
Manual-assisted analysis of one short policy with five to fifteen test cases, one revision comparison and a written report. Consider testing a fixed $150–$300 price only AFTER discussing buyer value and local legal authorization. These are proposed prices, not existing orders or an established pricing benchmark.

## Pricing learning
Identify frequency of changes, estimated hours/errors caused, approval chain and existing budget. Compare fixed service pilot to per-team or per-revision software subscription. Do not scale support promise before measuring direct delivery costs.

## Customer success
After each trial, record whether discovered issues were useful, whether policy changed, false alarms, ease of use and what customer did next. Retained customers actually rerun tests upon revision; being polite in a meeting is not retention.

## Marketing
One clean public fictional demo; honest case studies with explicit permission; occasional engineering/research write-up that reaches buyers; clear terms. Avoid premature product launch theater, paid campaigns and conference spending absent credible buyer acquisition.

## Competition posture
Recheck capabilities of Process Street, Scribe, Trainual, AWS Automated Reasoning, generic models, ordinary QA spreadsheets and bespoke internal tools on current dates. Win on a measured workflow advantage, not claims they cannot do.
